Studio notes

A new website for Trasier: lighter, clearer, and easier to use

We rebuilt our website from the ground up. Here is what changed, and why.

By Trasier TechnologyOctober 29, 2026
On this page 6 sections
  1. A lighter look
  2. A homepage that starts with us
  3. More room for Sadak Sujhav
  4. Easier to find your way
  5. Built to work for everyone
  6. What stayed the same

Our old website did its job, but it had grown one page at a time. Each page looked a little different, and the story of who we are and what we build was spread thin. So we rebuilt it. Every public page now shares one design, one navigation, and one clear way of telling our story.

A lighter look

The dark theme is gone. The new site uses white space, soft blue panels, and navy text, with clear blue buttons. It is calmer to read and easier on the eyes, on a laptop or a phone. Our owl logo now appears as it was designed, in blue and navy, with nothing around it.

A homepage that starts with us

The homepage now introduces Trasier first. At the top is an interactive 3D “T” that stands for what we do: digital products, intelligence, and connected systems. Three buttons beside it change its shape and show the matching kind of work, with links to our services. From there, the page walks through how we work and leads into our selected projects.

More room for Sadak Sujhav

Sadak Sujhav, our road-reporting app, has a fuller page of its own. It explains, step by step, how a citizen photographs a road problem, confirms the location, chooses how serious it is, and tracks the report as the road office works on it. The case study was rewritten too, with the problem, the approach, the public record, and answers to common questions in one place.

Easier to find your way

The main menu is now four plain links: Our work, What we do, About us, and Journal. Every page shares the same header and footer, so you always know where you are. The journal can now be filtered into product updates and studio notes, and the leadership page has proper profiles of the people behind Trasier.

Built to work for everyone

Movement on the site is gentle and short. If your device is set to reduce motion, the site respects that and shows everything still. All text is readable before any script loads, keyboard users can move through every page, and images have descriptions. The 3D scene pauses when it is off screen and falls back to a still picture on devices that cannot show it. There are no heavy outside scripts slowing pages down.

The contact and careers forms were also made more reliable. If a message ever cannot be sent, the form says so clearly and keeps what you typed, so nothing is lost.

What stayed the same

Every old link still works. The Sadak Sujhav privacy policy, terms of service, and account deletion pages are where they always were, and our earlier journal posts are all still here.

A website is often the first thing people see of a company. We wanted ours to feel like the products we build: simple, clear, and made with care in Nepal. If something looks wrong or could be better, tell us.
