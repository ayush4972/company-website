27,000 square metres in four months: Kathmandu's road crews, recognised

The Minister for Infrastructure Development has praised Road Division Kathmandu's repair work, and named Sadak Sujhav among the practices behind it.

By Trasier TechnologyNovember 20, 2026
On this page 5 sections
  1. What the minister said
  2. The work behind the number
  3. Repairs that happen at night
  4. Where Sadak Sujhav fits
  5. The people doing the work

This week, Er. Sunil Lamsal, Nepal's Minister for Infrastructure Development, shared a public post recognising the road repair work of Road Division Kathmandu. It is good news for everyone who drives, rides or walks on Kathmandu Valley's main roads. We are proud that Sadak Sujhav, the reporting app we built for the division, was named in it.

What the minister said

पछिल्लो चार महिनामा २७ हजार वर्गमिटरभन्दा बढी सडकका खाल्डाखुल्डी मर्मत तथा सुधार गरेर सडक डिभिजन काठमाडौँले उदाहरणीय काम गरेको छ ।

रोड एम्बुलेन्स, नियमित मर्मत सम्भार र ‘सडक सुझाव एप’ मार्फत नवीन अभ्यास सुरु गर्दै रात्रीकालीन समयमा समेत अहोरात्र खटिएको डिभिजनको काम प्रशंसनीय छ ।

काठमाडौँ उपत्यकाको सडकलाई सुरक्षित, व्यवस्थित र सुन्दर बनाउन खटिने सम्पूर्ण श्रमिक साथीहरू तथा सडक डिभिजन परिवारप्रति सम्मान ।

In English (our translation):

“In the last four months, Road Division Kathmandu has done exemplary work, repairing and improving potholes across more than 27,000 square metres of road.

The division’s work is commendable. It has started new practices through the Road Ambulance, regular maintenance and the ‘Sadak Sujhav app’, and has worked around the clock, including at night.

My respect to all the workers and the whole Road Division family who work to make the roads of Kathmandu Valley safe, orderly and beautiful.”

The work behind the number

According to Road Division Kathmandu, as reported by Ratopati, about 27,500 square metres of potholes were repaired and improved between Shrawan and Kartik, roughly mid-July to mid-November and the first four months of the fiscal year. The division did the work through two channels: its Road Ambulance and its regular maintenance contracts.

To put that in perspective, 27,500 square metres is close to four full-size football pitches of road surface, patched one pothole at a time.

Repairs that happen at night

The Road Ambulance is a quick-response repair unit that the division began running as a pilot in late 2025. It carries what a crew needs to cut, clean and fill a pothole on the spot. As the division explained when it started, urgent repairs on essential roads happen during the day, but most of the work is done at night, when traffic is light enough to close a lane safely. That is why the minister singled out the crews who work through the night.

Where Sadak Sujhav fits

Sadak Sujhav launched on 17 September 2026 as Road Division Kathmandu's channel for citizens to report problems on the federal roads it looks after in Kathmandu Valley. Anyone with the app can photograph a pothole or other road problem, confirm where it is, choose how serious it is, and send it straight to the division. They can then follow the report as it is handled.

For the crews, a report arrives with a photo and an exact location, so the right team can find it and plan the fix. For citizens, it replaces “someone should do something” with a report they can track.

Pothole · Samakhushi Chowk–Tokha · Kathmandu Division · repaired 9 October

Every completed repair in the app carries two photos: the one the citizen took when reporting the problem, and the one the road office took after the work was done. The pair above is one of the repairs from our two-month update.

Sadak Sujhav is one part of a bigger effort. The repairs themselves are the work of the division's engineers and crews, through the Road Ambulance and regular maintenance. The app's job is to make sure the problems people see on the road reach the people who can fix them.

The people doing the work

Behind every patched pothole is a crew working in the cold, often in the middle of the night, on roads that are busy again by morning. We join the minister in thanking every worker and engineer at Road Division Kathmandu.
