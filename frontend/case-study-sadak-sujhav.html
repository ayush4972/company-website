01 / The challenge

The road is visible.
The route to a response
should be, too.

Someone walking past a broken footpath can see the problem immediately. Knowing which office maintains that stretch, what information to send, and how to follow up is a different task.

The service brings the report and its context together. A photograph explains the issue; location and road-network information help establish where it belongs; a report history gives the person a place to return.

  1. 01 / RESPONSIBILITY

    Where should this go?

    Road ownership is not always obvious from the street. The reporting journey needs to connect a place with the office responsible for that road.

  2. 02 / USEFUL CONTEXT

    What does the office need?

    A general complaint is hard to locate. A photo, a precise map position, and severity make the observation more specific and easier to understand.

  3. 03 / FOLLOW-THROUGH

    What happened next?

    A submission should have a visible next step. Status updates and a report history help people follow the response without starting a new conversation each time.

At the public launch, OnlineKhabar described complaint tracking and information about the agency responsible for roads outside the federal network.

02 / The citizen journey

From noticing a problem
to following a report.

A small number of clear steps turns a street-level observation into a report with context.

  1. Show the problem.

    Photograph the pothole, damaged footpath, or road issue. The image gives the office a concrete starting point.

  2. Confirm the place.

    Use GPS or a nearby landmark to identify the location. Check that the map pin matches the affected stretch.

  3. Add the context.

    Choose the severity and review the road and office shown for the report before submitting.

  4. Submit the report.

    Send the photo and location together. The app links the report to road-network information for routing.

  5. Follow the response.

    Return to the report for status updates, notifications, and conversations with the road office.

A report has a life after submission.

This is a simplified status journey. The app can show additional states; a report does not automatically move through every stage.

Coverage is about responsibility.

A location on a map and an office actively handling reports are different things. Check the road and office shown in the app for the area you want to report.

A status is part of the record.

Report updates make the response easier to follow. Prioritization and repair work remain with the responsible road office; the software does not set a universal repair deadline.

Personal information has a purpose.

Photo and location information support the reporting process. The product privacy policy explains data handling, and the account-deletion page explains the available deletion route.

06 / Useful details

The questions
behind the report.

Practical answers for people using the app and teams exploring how a connected reporting service works.

Explore the product page
What kinds of issues can someone report?

The published app descriptions include potholes, cracks and other road damage, broken footpaths, and flooded road stretches. A useful report combines a photograph with location and severity information.

Does this cover every road in Nepal?

Coverage depends on road ownership and participating offices. Launch reporting focused on Kathmandu Valley’s federal roads, followed by dated company updates about Valley expansion. Review the matched road and road office in the app instead of assuming nationwide coverage.

Can a report be prepared without internet?

The October 17 Android release notes describe saving reports without a connection and sending them automatically when connectivity returns. Follow the app’s feedback to confirm whether a report has been submitted.

What happens after a report is submitted?

The app provides report history, status updates, and notifications. The published Android release also describes messaging with the road office inside the complaint, keeping follow-up associated with that report.

Does submitting a report guarantee a repair?

No repair time or outcome is guaranteed by submission. The road office assesses the issue, priorities, and the work to be carried out. The before-and-after photographs here are selected examples from the company’s field report.

Where can I read about privacy and account deletion?

Read the Sadak Sujhav privacy policy for the product’s published data practices and the account-deletion instructions for the available request process.

Can another public-service team discuss a similar platform?

Yes. Contact Trasier about a civic platform and describe the people, the service, the responsible teams, and the information that needs to flow between them.
