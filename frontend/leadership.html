Our story

Built in Kathmandu.
Built around people.

We started with an everyday frustration: a road issue without a clear path to the people who could address it. That question became the beginning of Trasier.

A practical starting point

Better public services need better connections.

That first question grew into Sadak Sujhav, a way to document road problems and connect reports with a service workflow. It also revealed the wider work: people need understandable tools, and institutions need dependable systems behind them.

Trasier brings those two perspectives together. We design for the person using the service and the team operating it, with equal care for both.

Our roots are in Nepal. The expectation we bring to every project is straightforward: useful software, thoughtful engineering, and clear communication.

An engineer's perspective

Close to the problem.
Involved in the details.

Ayush leads Trasier's day-to-day work and stays involved in architecture and delivery. His focus is connecting a client's actual problem to a product the team can build, operate, and improve.

The road reporting idea that became Sadak Sujhav shaped that approach: a good interface is only one part of a useful service. The data, ownership, and workflow behind it matter just as much.

Today, Trasier works across civic platforms, mobile and web products, mapping, AI, data, and integration. The common thread is making everyday systems easier to use and easier to run.
